ShotTitan

3D previs and virtual directing for filmmakers: block a scene, place actors and props, set up cameras and lenses, shoot your coverage, animate, light — and export an animatic.

ShotTitan runs in three ways, all with the same features:

  • Desktop app for Windows and macOS — works completely offline. Phone camera control is available here.
  • Web version — open the website and work in the browser, nothing to install.
  • Projects are plain .previs.json files you can move between both.

Install & first launch

Windows

  1. Download ShotTitan-Setup.exe from the download page.
  2. Run it. If Windows SmartScreen shows “Windows protected your PC”, click More info → Run anyway (the installer is not code-signed yet).
  3. Choose the install folder and finish. Start ShotTitan from the Start menu or the desktop shortcut.

macOS

  1. Download ShotTitan-mac.dmg (one build for Apple Silicon and Intel).
  2. Open it and drag ShotTitan into Applications.
  3. The first time, right-click the app → Open → Open. If macOS says the app “is damaged” or cannot be checked, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/ShotTitan.app" in Terminal.

Web version

Open the web app link from the download page in Chrome, Edge or Safari (a computer with a reasonably modern graphics card is recommended). Your projects are saved in that browser.

Home

The app opens on Home, not on the last film. On the left: New film (title and first scene), Sample film (a furnished living room with two actors and three shots), From a screenplay (a new film, then the Screenplay → Film window) and Open file…. On the right: Continue (the film you worked on last) and All films, newest first, with search and sorting by date or name. Each card shows the cover, scenes, shots, length and when it was last edited; its ⋯ menu has Open, Rename, Duplicate and Delete. Drop a .previs.json file on Home to open it. Tick Open the last film at launch to skip Home. Home also shows the creator — Saeed Salarvand — with his Instagram, Telegram and LinkedIn; the “Crafted by” credit in the bottom-right corner opens the About card.

Updates

The desktop app checks for a new version each time it starts (and every few hours). Windows: the update downloads in the background, then Restart and update installs it — or it installs when you close the app. macOS: you are told a new version is out and Download opens it (macOS only lets signed apps replace themselves). Help → Check for Updates… checks now. Your projects are kept.

The five pages

The bar at the bottom of the window has the five pages, in the order a film is made: Write · Stage · Camera · Edit · Deliver (Alt+1…5). The 3D view is the same on every page — only what is around it changes — and the top bar (film, scene, search, undo, tools) stays on all of them.

Search (Ctrl+K, or the magnifier in the top bar) finds any command, page, shot, camera, object or scene by typing a few letters; Enter runs it. Right-click works on the things you see: in the 3D view (frame it, new camera on it, where the move starts or ends, alternate angle, keys on/off), on a row of the scene list (frame it, new camera on it, walk path, duplicate, hide, delete), on a shot (look through, alternate angle, keys, duplicate, delete) and on the edit timeline. + Camera in the top bar adds a camera from the view or framed on someone, each with its shot.

Building a scene

  1. Add things: double-click an asset, or drag it onto the floor, a table, a shelf… it lands on the surface under the cursor.
  2. Place them: select, then W move · E rotate · R scale (Q back to select). S toggles snapping, X world/local axes, End drops the selection onto what is below.
  3. Solid world (brick-wall button in the toolbar, on by default): nothing passes through anything. A character or piece of furniture you drag stops at walls and furniture and slides along them (stacking on top still works); a walk you key or draw goes round what is in the way (detour keys are added and re-planned when you change the walk); the Director never places people inside furniture and keeps them clear of walls; arms and hands ease back instead of going into a wall, a table or another person. Turn it off to overlap things on purpose.
  4. Organise: Ctrl+D duplicate, Ctrl+G group, H hide, lock in the Scene list, rename in the Inspector.
  5. Give life: in the Inspector, Animation adds a looping action (idle, talk, sit, type, wave, walk in place, wheels turning, birds flying…). To make things travel from A to B, see Animating characters & vehicles.
Tip. Large spaces work: buildings, towers, roads, military vehicles and street kits are in the library, and the far clipping distance reaches several kilometres.

Model look & picture quality

  • Clay / Color (palette button in the toolbar): Clay shows every library model in one slightly dark off-white, like an architectural model — shapes read through light, shadow and soft contact shadows. Color shows the models in their own colors. Asset thumbnails always show colors.
  • Wall and ceiling pieces: wall cabinets, range hoods, mirrors, pictures, sconces, a wall-mounted TV or blinds go straight to their usual height when dropped on the floor; pendants, chandeliers, ceiling lights and ceilings hang from a 2.8 m ceiling. Drop them on a table or shelf and they stay there.
  • Replace a model: select it, then Inspector → Model → Replace… shows models of the same family first (another sofa, another tree, another lamp), then similar ones, then the rest of the category. Position, rotation, size, color and per-shot changes stay. Tick All … in the scene to swap every copy at once (e.g. all dining chairs). Right-click a model → Replace with… does the same from the 3D view. Undo with Ctrl+Z.
  • Color of one model: select it, then in the Inspector Color pick a swatch or + for any color (works on several selected models at once). Default gives it back the Clay / Color look.
  • Picture quality (Low · Medium · High · Ultra, next to the palette button): model detail (rounded edges, soft cushions, smoother curves), soft contact shadows (ambient occlusion), shadow sharpness and pixel density. Use Low on a weak graphics card, Ultra on a strong one. It is a view setting: projects do not change, and animatic / storyboard exports use the same quality.

Importing your own models

Bring in your own 3D models — .glb, .gltf (self-contained), .fbx or .obj. Click Import at the top of the Assets panel, or drop the file onto the window.

  • Size: files in centimetres or millimetres are recognised and converted to metres; type the real height to change it. The model stands on the floor, centred.
  • Look: keep the model’s own materials, or use the clay look of the rest of the scene.
  • Kind: prop, character, animal, vehicle or architecture — where it appears in the Scene list and how the AI prompts describe it.
  • Imported models are in Assets → My models for every project and scene, and work in reusable sets. The file is stored once; a saved project file includes it, so it opens on any computer.
  • Imported models are static: move them with keyframes (K) or Draw path — walk cycles are for the built-in characters.
Note. Draco-compressed files and .gltf files with separate texture files are not supported yet: export as .glb without Draco compression.

Reference images

  • Over the camera frame — select nothing so the shot settings show in the Inspector, then Reference → Add image…: a storyboard panel, a frame from a film you want to match, a location photo. In camera view (C) it lies over the frame; set its opacity, Fit (whole image) or Fill (cover the frame), hide it with the eye, replace or remove it. Each shot has its own; it is saved with the project and undoable.
  • As a panel in the scene — Import in the Assets panel (or drop the file on the window) also takes .png, .jpg, .webp: the image stands on the floor as a double-sided panel, 1.5 m high by default. Use it as a backdrop, a view outside a window, or a mood board next to the set; it is in My models like imported models.

Cameras & lenses

Add a camera exactly where you are looking with Shift+C. Select a camera to set in the Inspector: focal length, sensor / film back, aspect ratio (2.39, 2.35, 1.85, 16:9, 4:3, 3:2, 1:1, 4:5, 9:16), focus distance and aperture, clipping. [ and ] cycle through cameras. The camera view shows the exact frame with safe areas and guides.

Shots

A shot is a camera plus a duration and its own adjustments. Press N (or New shot) to create a shot from the active camera, 1–9 to switch, 0 for the base scene.

  • All shots / This shot (top right): choose whether moving an object changes it everywhere, or only in the active shot (a “cheat” for this angle). Changed objects show a badge with Reset to scene.
  • Each shot keeps its own size, angle, notes, lighting tweaks and exposure — the shot list is built for you.

Framing a shot (Frame the shot)

Say what the shot is and the camera goes there. With a shot active (or its camera selected) the inspector shows Frame the shot: On (who or what — the person in view is picked for you), Size (EWS … ECU), Angle (eye, high, low, top, Dutch, ground), Side (front, ¾ left/right, side left/right, back — left and right are the subject’s), Lens (auto or 18–135 mm: the camera comes closer or stands back to keep the size). A wall or furniture behind the camera brings it in and widens the lens.

  • Start here / End here: the move starts (or ends) where the view is now — look through the camera and move it, or use the editor view. With no move yet the other end is the camera as it is set up, so there is a move straight away.
  • If the camera already moves in the shot, a change asks what it should do: Move the whole move (every key shifts by the same amount: the same move, reframed), Key it at the playhead, or Switch the keys off. Moving a keyed camera by hand shows the same choice over the view.
  • Alternate angle: a new camera on the same moment from the other side, one size tighter, cut in right after the shot.
  • Keys on / off: see below.

Film, scenes & sets

A film is made of sequences, sequences of scenes, scenes of shots. Every scene has its own 3D set, lighting and time of day, cast, keyframes, shots and undo. The film button in the toolbar (next to the scene name, e.g. Sc 2/5) lists every scene; click one to open it. Projects you made before films each became a film of their own.

  • New scene… — name, sequence, and an empty set or a set from the library. Duplicate this scene copies set, cast, lights and shots. Add a project as a scene… copies another project into this film (the original stays as it is).
  • Film timeline (Shift+F) — the whole film in one row: sequences, coloured scenes and their shots sized by duration, numbered scene.shot (3.2), with the running length. Click a shot to open its scene at that shot. Drag a scene’s header to reorder it, also into another sequence. Double-click names to rename. The film’s own undo / redo buttons undo order and name changes.
  • Play film plays shot after shot and opens the next scene when one ends. Export film animatic (or Whole film / a sequence in the animatic dialog) renders every scene into one video.

Reusable sets

Build a location once and use it in many scenes — the same bedroom at night in scene 1 and in daylight in scene 5, each with its own lighting, actors and animation.

  1. Open the Set library (film menu or the film timeline), name the set and click Save set. By default architecture, furniture & props and vehicles are saved; lights and characters stay in the scene unless you tick them.
  2. In New scene… pick the set, or click Place in scene in the library. The items are linked to the set: move, change or delete them freely in that scene — only that scene changes.
  3. To change the set itself, edit it in a scene that uses it and choose New version of “…” → Save new version. Other scenes show “The set has a new version”: Update scene applies it (Ctrl+Z undoes it) and keeps what that scene changed itself; Later asks again next time; Detach makes the items the scene’s own.
Tip. Models are shared, not copied: a set placed in ten scenes stores only the list of its items in each scene.

Timeline & keyframes

  1. Put the playhead at 0 s, frame the shot, press K.
  2. Move the playhead, change the framing, press K again. Press Space to play.

Drag diamonds to retime them, Delete removes, Ctrl+C/V copies camera keys, the Interpolation menu sets Smooth / Linear / Ease / Hold. Auto Key (Shift+K) keys every camera change automatically. Drag the end marker to change the shot length. ←/→ step one frame, Shift+←/→ jump between keys. The filter button at the right of the timeline toolbar keeps rows for the selected objects only (as in Blender); other animated objects fold into one line — click it to show them all. The film strip above can be hidden with its arrow (or Shift+F); its ⋯ menu has sequences, the set library, exports and the film’s undo.

Camera keys on / off (like a stopwatch): the Keys on button in the keys timeline (and the ⏱ button of the Edit timeline for the whole scene) switches the camera keyframes off without deleting them — for this shot or for every shot of the scene. Off, the camera holds its set-up, changes go to the set-up, and the move on the edit clip is crossed out; switch it on and the move is back.

Editing (the Edit timeline)

The tabs above the bottom panel switch between Keys (the active shot’s keyframes; Space plays and loops that shot) and Edit — a timeline like an editing program’s. In Edit, Space plays the whole edit: each shot follows the one before with its own camera, the music runs on through the cuts, and dialogue is heard under the picture that shows it. The Product Ad studio opens the Edit timeline by itself.

  • V1 holds the shots end to end (thumbnails, the camera’s keyframes as small diamonds). A1, A2… hold the sounds, with waveforms. Each track header has lock, mute (M), solo (S), volume and a level meter; double-click a name to rename it; Add audio track adds Dialogue, Music or Effects.
  • Selection (A): drag a clip to move it in the edit (Alt-drag copies it); drag its right end to trim the tail, its left end to trim the head — what follows closes up (ripple), and a trimmed head keeps the picture on the same moment of the action and of the camera move.
  • Trim (T): drag a cut to roll it (one shot longer, the next shorter); drag inside a clip to slip it (same place and length, an earlier or later moment).
  • Blade (B): click a clip or a sound to cut it there. Ctrl+B cuts at the playhead.
  • Snapping (N) to cuts, sound edges, markers, the playhead and the music’s beats; hold Shift to drag freely. Ctrl+wheel zooms, Shift+Z fits the edit, the rows button changes the track height.
  • Sounds: drag to move (or to another track), drag the ends to trim, the white squares at the top corners for fades, the line across for the level. Right-click for fades, Crossfade into the next sound, level 100%.
  • Right-click a clip: cut here, duplicate, delete, Transition in (Cut, Dissolve, Dip to black, Wipe — 0.25 to 2 s), colour label, open its keyframes. Transitions show live in camera view and are mixed into the exported video.
  • Markers (M) on the ruler: drag to move, double-click to rename, right-click for colour. In / Out (I, O; Alt+X clears): playing stays between them (with Loop on it loops).
  • The Media Pool on the left. Project: the film like an editing program’s bins — every sequence is a folder with its scenes (+ Sequence makes one, + on a folder adds a scene, drag a scene onto another folder to move it, double-click to rename, click a scene to open it; the open scene lists its shots). Cameras: a picture of what each camera sees, a name that says it (size · who · lens), which shots use it; click the picture to look through it, double-click the name to rename, Insert / Append or drag onto V1. Sounds: import, or drag onto a track.
  • Gaps: drag a clip along V1 and it leaves black time behind it; drop it on another clip to put it in that place instead. Right-click a clip → Insert a 1 s gap before; click a gap and Delete closes it; ⋯ → Close all gaps.
  • + Shot (toolbar of the timeline, or the + at the end of V1): a new shot from the view or from any camera, at the playhead or at the end. The less-used tools (Trim, In / Out, loop, zoom, track height) are in ⋯.
  • Each clip shows its camera move (e.g. ➚ Dolly in · Pan R), worked out from its keys.
  • Delete / Backspace removes what is selected: clips (the rest close up), sounds, markers. Deleting the ad’s music removes the music. Everything is one undo step.
  • Box selection: drag on empty space to select the clips, sounds and markers under the box (Shift adds). Dragging the playhead plays short bites of the sound (scrub).
  • Volume keyframes: Alt+click a sound’s level line to add a point, drag points to shape the level, Alt+click a point to delete it (right-click → Clear volume keyframes).
  • Speed (right-click a clip → Speed, 25%–400%): slow motion or faster; the clip’s length on the edit follows, and sound linked to it plays at the same rate.
  • The whole film on one timeline: every scene follows the one before (a strip of scene names above V1), the open scene is bright, the others dim — click one to open it. Space plays the film from the playhead through all its scenes.
  • When every shot starts in the action where it starts in the edit (one take, an ad) every track is free on the edit clock; in a cut film a dialogue track is Linked to the pictures and a music track is Free — the button in the track header switches it.

Dialogue (voices)

Characters can really speak. Dialogue (right of the Keys / Edit tabs) opens the scene’s lines: who says what, when and how. A line gets its sound in one of three ways, and it goes on the Voices track, under the shots that show that moment:

  • Voice it — the built-in voice (Kokoro, English, 24 voices) speaks the line on this computer, free and offline. The first time it downloads its model (~90 MB) and keeps it. Voice all does every line without sound (or changed since).
  • Record — your own voice with the microphone (press again to stop).
  • File — a sound file (MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, FLAC) for the line.

Each line has a speaker (or an off-screen voice), a start time (seconds of the scene; ⇤ Playhead puts it at the playhead), its words, an emotion (calm, happy, excited, sad, angry, scared, whisper, shout) and a pace. The built-in voice has no emotion control of its own: the emotion is given through pace, level and punctuation. Character voices sets each character’s voice and pace (▶ hears it); new characters get different voices that match the figure.

On the Edit timeline, right-click a clip → Add dialogue here…; lines without sound show as dashed boxes where they are said, and double-clicking a voiced line’s sound opens it. While a line plays, the speaker’s head moves a little with the voice (in exported videos too). Voiced lines are ordinary sounds: move, trim, fade them; deleting the sound keeps the line.

Importing a screenplay asks Spoken dialogue? before building: Yes voices every line (a parenthetical such as “(whispering)” or “(angry)” becomes the emotion); No keeps the lines in each scene without sound, to voice later.

Camera motion

The Camera Motion tab designs the shot’s camera move in three steps:

  1. Subject — what the camera is about: a character, any object or a null target (pick it from the list with pictures, click Pick and click it in the view, or Selected). Then where it sits in the frame: Center, Left ⅓, Right ⅓, Eyes ⅓ (upper third) or Custom.
  2. Move — a preset (dolly in / out, push in, pan, tilt, truck, pedestal, crane, jib, boom, arc, orbit, circular, zoom, dolly zoom, follow, lead, side follow, over-the-shoulder, parallax, reveal, handheld, static), or Describe it: type the move in plain words, for example “slow push in on Sarah until a close-up”, “orbit around the null target 180 degrees at 3 meters, low angle”, “crane up from her hands to a wide shot of the room”, “rack focus from Anna to the window”, “static low angle on David, 35mm”. Read shows how it was understood (subject, move, numbers, rig, lens); words it did not understand are shown in amber, never ignored. Fix anything below, then Apply. (No AI is needed; with your Claude key, Ask Claude to rephrase it helps with unusual wording.)
  3. Options — every move has its own: an orbit has radius, start angle, sweep, direction, height, height change and look offset; a push has distance or an end size (“until a close-up”); a crane has start height, rise, reach, arc; a follow has distance, height, side offset and lag; a dolly zoom has its start and end lens. Every move also has start size, camera height (eye level, low, high, overhead), duration (0 = the whole shot), pace, easing, rig feel, a lens change, focus on the subject or a rack focus, and dutch. The plan above the options shows the move from above (green = start, red = end, yellow = the subject). Apply writes ordinary camera keys you can fine-tune on the timeline.

The camera first frames the subject as asked, then moves around it: the orbit circles it at the radius, the push goes toward it, the crane rises keeping it framed. A subject that moves (someone walking, a keyed null) is followed: it stays on its point of the frame. After Apply the move stays editable: change an option and it is made again at once (one undo step). If you changed its keys by hand since, Apply asks before replacing them (or offers to reshape your keys instead). Right-click a character or object in the view for Camera: orbit around this and Camera: push in on this.

Null targets are points to aim at: a cross with its name, seen only in the editor (never in shots, thumbnails or exports). Add one from + Camera → Null target, from the subject list (New null target here) or with a right-click in the view (Add null target here). Move, rotate and key it like any object; put one between two people, key it to slide, and orbit around it. Right-click a character → Parent null to this puts a null on them, their head or a hand, so it follows them.

Parenting: anything can follow anything — a null on a hand, a lamp on a car. In the scene list drag a row onto another, or right-click → Parent to… / Clear parent; the Inspector has a Parent section (with Pick and the joint). Things keep their place when parented or freed.

Below the three steps, cinematic layers change how the keys play without touching them:

LayerWhat it does
TimingEase in/out, constant speed, speed ramps, holds
PathShape the A→B move into an arc, orbit, crane or straight line
Smoothing · PolishRemove bumps; analyse and fix jerky moves
Tracking · CompositionKeep a subject in frame, on a chosen screen position
Rig · ShakeTripod, handheld, shoulder, gimbal, steadicam, dolly, slider, crane, jib, drone, car mount, cable cam; camera shake by style

Bake writes the result into plain keyframes; Restore brings the original take back.

Draw Path (camera)

  1. Click Draw Path (or Shift+D). The view switches to the top of the scene.
  2. Draw the route with the left mouse button. Shift+drag continues the same line. Right / middle drag pans, wheel zooms.
  3. Set the start and end height, where the camera looks (Along path, At subject — choose a character, or Fixed), the duration, Ease or Constant speed, smoothing and Bank (drone-style roll in turns).
  4. Apply path (or Enter) writes the keys on the active shot’s camera (a new camera and shot if none is active). Esc cancels.

Animating characters & vehicles

With keyframes

  1. Select the character (or car), put the playhead where the move starts and press K (or Key here in the Inspector’s Movement section).
  2. Move the playhead, move the object with the gizmo — once an object has keys, moving it writes a key at the playhead automatically.
  3. Press Space. The object travels between its keys.

With a drawn route

In Movement, click Draw path, draw where it should go (it starts from where it stands), pick a speed — Sneak / Walk / Jog / Run for people, 15–80 km/h for cars — and Apply route. Draw again from a later playhead to chain moves (“walk to the table, then run to the door”).

What happens automatically

  • Gait by speed: standing still → idle, slow → walk, faster → jog, fast → run, with smooth transitions. Feet do not slide: the steps follow the distance travelled. Or choose a fixed gait in Gait.
  • Face travel: the body turns towards where it goes, and keeps facing that way when it stops. Turn it off to control rotation with keys.
  • Vehicles: wheels roll by distance and the front wheels steer into turns; parked cars straighten their wheels.
  • Between moves, a character plays its own animation (for example Talk or Wave) if it has one.
  • The action is shared by every shot (continuity across your coverage). In a shot’s settings, Action from lets a shot start later in the action.

Changes of action over time

In the Inspector’s Animation section, Changes over time → At playhead makes a character do something else from that moment of the scene on: stands talking, then sits at 6 s, then stands up and waves at 12 s. Change the time or the action in the list, or remove it (all undoable). Walking between keyed marks stays automatic — the action plays whenever the character stands still. The Director and Screenplay → Film write these changes too (“walks to the sofa and sits”).

Sound (dialogue, music, effects)

In the timeline, Add sound → Dialogue / Music / Effects puts an audio file (MP3, WAV, M4A/AAC, OGG, FLAC) at the playhead. Sounds live on the scene’s continuous time — the same clock the actors move on — so every shot hears the part of the scene it shows, and a line stays in sync across cuts.

  • Each track has M (mute), S (solo) and a volume slider; a sound already on a track makes the next one go to a new track of the same kind.
  • Drag a sound left / right to move it in time, up / down to another track; hover it and click × to delete. Everything is undoable.
  • Playback plays the sound with the picture; Export animatic (scene, sequence or whole film) writes it into the video.
  • Sound files are kept once in this browser / computer and saved inside project files.

Lighting

  • Setups: one click places a full lighting setup around the subject and camera — three-point, high-key, low-key, Rembrandt, split, butterfly, silhouette, golden hour, moonlight, window light, neon and firelight.
  • Lights: spot, point, area and sun; colour in Kelvin, intensity in stops (±1 stop buttons), softness, beam, shadows, effects (flicker, candle, fire, TV, pulse, police, lightning). Shift+L adds a spot.
  • Practicals: lamps, chandeliers, sconces and other fixtures can switch on their own light (Inspector → Switch on light).
  • Time of day moves the sun and sky; Exposure brightens or darkens the whole picture. Both can be set per shot.
  • Solo a light to see what it does alone.

Director (AI scene building)

The Director has one box for everything: a few lines about a scene (“A café at night. Emma sits by the window. Tom comes in and walks to her. ‘Sorry I’m late,’ Tom says.”), a whole screenplay, or a script file (Open script file, or drop it on the box). It reads which one it is — One scene or Screenplay (you can switch) — and shows what it read. Open it with D anywhere, or go to the Write page, where it is the page itself.

  • Plan with Claude, Copy for Claude chat (no API key), or Plan without AI (the built-in screenplay reader: a description is read as a one-scene screenplay — people, places, time, quoted lines as dialogue).
  • One preview for both, then Build into: This scene, Add to this scene or New scene in the film for a scene; New film or Add to this film for a screenplay. When there are spoken lines it asks whether to give them voices. Refine the plan in plain words first if you like.

Connecting Claude

  1. Click the status pill at the top right of the Director (Connect Claude…).
  2. Paste your Anthropic API key (create one in the Claude Console — the link is in the panel) and click Save key. The key is checked, then stored: in the desktop app it is encrypted by the operating system; in the web version it stays in that browser.
  3. Choose the model: Opus 5.5 (best plans), Sonnet 5.5 (balanced) or Haiku 5.5 (fastest, lowest cost).
Note. Claude is the only part that needs internet. Usage is billed to your own Anthropic account. When the app runs inside claude.ai, your Claude account is used and no key is needed.

Without an API key: your Claude chat

Already pay for Claude (Pro / Max)? Use it directly — no API key, no extra cost:

  1. Write the prompt (or fill the brief) and click Copy for Claude chat. The full instruction — format, rules, the asset catalogue and your request — is copied.
  2. Paste it into a new chat on claude.ai or in the Claude app and send it.
  3. Copy Claude’s whole reply, paste it into the box that opened in the Director, and click Read plan. Review it and Build scene.
  4. To change something, type it in the Refine field and click Copy for chat: the current plan is included, so Claude revises it instead of starting over.
Tip — where a shot starts. Say it in the prompt (“the camera starts just behind Emma’s shoulder and dollies to Tom”). The plan then gives the shot a starting point ("from": {"behind": "emma"}), so the camera begins over that person’s shoulder instead of wherever the shot size would put it.
Wild wall. Rooms the Director builds have an open front so you can film them from outside, like a stage. That front is a wild wall: from inside the room it is a normal wall (a close-up looking towards the front sees a wall, not black space); from outside it is see-through and casts no shadow.

AI Frames

Turn any moment of the scene into a finished storyboard frame with AI — your scene stays the reference, it is not just a prompt. Press AI Frames in the toolbar (a separate window; nothing else changes).

  • Reference: the active shot camera or the current view, at the playhead. The app renders the frame (flat light by default), an exact depth map, a region map (one colour per object) and line art, and knows who stands where. Refresh after moving the playhead or the view.
  • Model: MOSS (MOSS-Image with its colour/depth control), Flux (Flux.1 Depth — composition from the depth map) or Qwen (Qwen Image Edit redraws the frame itself and can take actors' face photos).
  • Style: ten looks — pencil sketch, ink & marker, charcoal, cinematic photo, film noir, 3D animated film, anime, comic book, watercolor, concept painting. The thumbnails are previews made from your own frame until you press Make the 10 style samples in Settings (then they are real AI samples, per model).
  • Faces (optional): a photo per actor in frame (used by Qwen). Notes (optional): what the frame cannot show. The words that go with the frame are written for you from the scene.
  • Variants 1–4, then Generate. Frames stay with the scene; ★ chooses a frame for its shot, and the storyboard PDF option AI frames prints it instead of the 3D render. With make the missing ones (model and style chosen right there) every shot without a ★ frame gets one made during the export, from its shot camera.

Settings: the ComfyUI address (your own GPU — free and unlimited — or a rented one such as RunPod). Start ComfyUI with --enable-cors-header. The app reads which nodes and model files the server has, picks the files by name and says exactly what is missing. You can replace a model's workflow with your own (ComfyUI → Workflow → Export (API)) using the tokens {{prompt}}, {{negative}}, {{color}}, {{depth}}, {{regions}}, {{edges}}, {{face1}}, {{face2}}, {{seed}}, {{width}}, {{height}}, {{hold}}. Without ComfyUI the rest of the app works as before.

Screenplay → Film

A whole screenplay goes in the same Director box (it is read as a Screenplay); the film menu’s Screenplay → Film… and Home’s From a screenplay open the Director for it. Any language, any format; the result is in English.

Or press Open script file (or drop the file on the text box): PDF, Final Draft (.fdx), Fountain, Word (.docx) or plain text. Persian and Arabic PDFs are read right to left. A scanned PDF with no text layer is added as page images (up to 20) for Claude to read — with your API key; on a Claude chat, attach the pages yourself. Check the text before planning.

  1. Plan with Claude (API key or claude.ai), or Copy for Claude chat → paste into your Claude chat → paste the answer → Read plan. You can add storyboard images: they are sent with your API key; in a Claude chat, attach them yourself.
  2. Check the plan: every scene with its heading, sequence, location, cast and shots. Places used by several scenes are marked shared set.
  3. Choose New film or Add to this film and click Build film. Each scene is built in turn: its set, cast, lighting and time of day, timed blocking (people wait, then walk / jog / run to their marks at the given seconds) and shots that start where their moment happens. A place used again reuses the same set (linked: set updates reach every scene).
Tip. Each scene keeps its own undo: Ctrl+Z in a scene undoes its build. Refine any scene afterwards with the Director, or by hand.

Writing a screenplay the app reads exactly

The app reads the standard screenplay format (the master-scene format of Final Draft, Fountain, Highland, WriterDuet — and the same in Persian) and breaks the script down like a script supervisor: scenes, places, times, who is in each scene, the dialogue in order and the shots the script asks for. The window shows what it read (Read as a screenplay: … scenes · locations · characters · length) and warns about what is missing. The closer the script follows the standard, the more exact the film:

  • Scene heading on its own line: INT. / EXT. (داخلی / خارجی) — the place — the time (DAY, NIGHT, DAWN, DUSK, MORNING, EVENING, CONTINUOUS, LATER, or a clock time 21:30; روز، شب، صبح، عصر، غروب، ادامه). A scene number may come first (12., ۱., صحنه ۳).
  • Write a place that comes back exactly the same every time — it becomes one shared set. A room inside a place goes after a dash: HOUSE - KITCHEN.
  • Introduce each character the first time with age (and gender if the name does not say it): EMMA (30s), a tired nurse, جک (مرد، ۴۰ ساله). The app picks the actor (man, woman, boy, girl, teen, elderly) from it.
  • Action in the present tense, what we see: who sits, stands, walks where, talks on the phone.
  • Character cue above the dialogue (English: in capitals; Persian: the name alone on its line, or نام:), extensions (V.O.) (O.S.) (CONT'D); a parenthetical (quietly) under the cue.
  • Shot directions only where they matter: CLOSE ON, ANGLE ON, INSERT, POV, WIDE (نمای نزدیک، نمای باز، اینسرت، نقطه دید). Transitions on their own line: CUT TO:, DISSOLVE TO: (قطع به:). Acts or sequences as Fountain sections # ACT ONE; notes as [[ … ]].
INT. HOSPITAL - NURSES' STATION - NIGHT

EMMA (30s), a tired nurse, sits at the desk. She looks up.

DAVID (60s) shuffles in, holding a paper cup.

                    DAVID
          (quietly)
          You're still here.

CLOSE ON the paper cup.

                                        CUT TO:

EXT. HOSPITAL PARKING LOT - CONTINUOUS

Build from the script (no AI) builds the whole film straight from what was read: a furnished set per place (kitchen, living room, bedroom, office, café, street, park, beach…), the cast by age and gender, people seated or standing facing whoever they talk to, lighting from the time of day, and dialogue coverage — a master, over-the-shoulder shots for two people, singles for a group, every shot the script asks for — timed to the dialogue. Plan with Claude gets the same breakdown with the script and must follow it scene for scene; the plan is checked against it (missing scenes or people are listed).

Exporting an animatic

Project menu → Export animatic (video)… or Ctrl+E. Choose 720p, 1080p or 4K, 24/25/30 fps, which shots, and burn-ins (shot, camera and lens, shot timecode, running timecode, project name). Every shot is rendered through its own camera, one after another, letterboxed to its aspect ratio. The result is an MP4 (H.264) file — or WebM where H.264 is unavailable. Rendering is frame-exact, so even a slow computer produces a perfectly timed video.

Storyboard (PDF)

Project menu → Export storyboard (PDF)… (Ctrl+Shift+E), or the storyboard button in the film timeline for the whole film. Every shot becomes a panel rendered through its own camera, numbered scene.shot, with its length, shot size, angle, lens, camera move, who is in frame and what they do, and your shot notes (Persian and other scripts print correctly). Shots where the camera or an actor moves get their last frame as a small inset.

  • What: this scene, the whole film or one sequence. Each scene starts on a new page.
  • Panels per page: 1, 2, 3 (a shot list: image and text side by side), 4 or 6 · A4 or Letter · landscape or portrait.
  • Show: camera & action, notes, end frame.

Product Ad studio

Switch the toolbar from Film to Product Ad for product commercials. 60 products in 10 categories (fragrance, makeup, personal care, drinks, food, tech, watches & jewelry, fashion, home, sport & tools) — Products has search and category filters; each has size, width and height and a material per part. The perfume bottle is the detailed one (cap, label, part animations). Any imported model can also be used as a product (Products → Use selected object as product).

  1. Products → New product ad: a new project with a studio stage, the bottle, a hero camera and luxury lighting.
  2. Templates: pick a length (6, 10, 15 or 30 s) and a frame, then Build. You get real, editable shots — each with its camera move, product animation and text. One undo step.
  3. Music edit — the ad cut to a song. Add music… (MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, FLAC): it is analysed for its tempo (BPM), beats, bars, sections (intro, build-up, drop, main, break, outro), the drop and the final hit, all drawn on its waveform. Cut and fit the music: drag the start / end handles (they snap to bar lines), pick a length (6–60 s or any) — whole bars are cut out on phrase lines (the start, the drop and the song’s own ending are kept) or a phrase of the loudest part is repeated to make it longer — with Keep the song’s own ending, fades, volume and Listen. Cut to the music: the music chooses the style (or you do), the moves and how many (or use your own moves, in order): cuts land on the beat and on bar lines, quiet parts get long gliding shots, loud parts short punchy ones, the build-up cuts faster and faster into the drop, the drop opens on a punch-in exactly on the beat, and the last shot is always the packshot, landing on the final hit. 3 versions…: on the music · faster, cutting on the hits · long flowing shots with combined moves. Shots · edit by hand: every camera move is one shot — move it earlier / later, swap its move, make it a beat longer or shorter or type its length, duplicate or delete it; the music plays on through the edit; then Re-cut to the music puts every cut back on the beat and fits the edit to the music. The packshot always stays last (even when dragged).
  4. Auto edit: pick camera moves (any number, in order — or none and the style picks), a length and a style (luxury, energetic, tech, minimal), then Build the edit: the whole ad is cut into shots. Options: one continuous take, cut on the music’s beat (with music). Every edit ends on a packshot — always the last shot. 3 versions… shows three cuts of the same choice. 9:16 / 16:9 / 1:1 / 4:5 copy makes the same ad in another frame as a new scene. Plan with Claude (free): describe the ad, Copy prompt into a Claude chat, paste the answer and Build from the answer (or Ask Claude now with your API key). Between shots: as the style likes, cuts, match cuts (the next shot starts at the angle the last one ended on, a size closer or wider) or whip pans. The edit follows the rules of commercial cutting: the shot size changes at every cut (wide, medium, close, macro) or the angle by 30° or more — no jump cuts — one screen direction, the camera keeps moving through the cuts and only the last shot settles, orbits keep a calm pace for the shot length, medium shots sit on the thirds, and when no moves are picked the style tells a story (tease, reveal, hero, details, energy, packshot).
  5. Product tab: shape, size, cap, liquid level, label; a material per part (glass, gold, chrome…); upload label artwork and a cap logo; save it to My Library.
  6. Animate: 30 product animations for this product (the thumbnails show the actual motion). Click to apply and preview. Adjust start, duration, speed, intensity, easing, direction, loop, and blend a continuous float on top. Presets that do not fit (e.g. two-product moves with one product) are flagged before they are applied.
  7. Camera: 60 moves in groups (dolly & slide, orbit, up & down, pan & tilt, lens & focus, macro, combined & feel), framed from the product’s size; hover a card to see the move. When the shot already has a move you choose: Add after (the moves play one after another — the shot gets longer), Replace, or Combine (both at once, e.g. orbit + push-in). Every move in the list is editable: length, speed curve (incl. speed ramp), lens, amount, distance, height, aperture, direction, and for the 2nd+ move Continue (carry on from where the camera is) or Own framing; reorder or remove moves. The keys on the timeline are rebuilt each time. Angle turns the whole move round the product and In frame puts the product on the left or right third. Each product starts from its best side (a watch or a shoe from three-quarters, a teapot in profile).
  8. Light / Lighting: 16 studio looks of real lights; backdrop, floor (matte, gloss, mirror), reflections, your own HDRI.
  9. Brand: brand name, colours, font, logo; add headline, price, call to action… with entrance / exit animations. They show over the camera frame (C) with safe areas and are burned into exports.
  10. Effects: light sweep, particles, glow, background colour change, shadow reveal, vignette, condensation.
  11. Export: commercial video, storyboard PDF, AI prompts & frames, shot list CSV, ad JSON, products GLB, frames up to 4K, transparent PNG, PNG sequence.
Not yet: rendered depth of field, motion trails, a shared online library (share presets as pack files from Library).

AI prompt & frames

Turn a previs shot into the inputs an AI video generator needs: a clean first frame and last frame through the shot camera, and a prompt in English written from what is really in your scene. Click AI Prompt in the toolbar (Shift+A).

  • What it reads from the shot: shot size (from how much of the main person the frame shows), camera angle and height, focal length, the camera move (dolly, truck, pan, tilt, crane, arc, zoom, handheld) and its speed, who is in frame, where, which way they face, what they do and where they walk, over-the-shoulder framing, rack focus, the lights and time of day, and your shot notes. Nothing is invented.
  • Presets: Text to video, Image to video, Seedance, Kling, Veo and Runway. They change the order and emphasis (Runway: short, motion only, no negative prompt). Image-to-video presets describe only what moves — attach the exported first frame.
  • Style: your own words (“cinematic, 35mm film, moody”), kept separate from the scene facts.
  • Edit the prompt freely (regenerate brings back the generated one), or Edit template to rewrite a preset with placeholders such as {{shot}}, {{camera}}, {{subjects}}, {{lighting}}.
  • Copy prompt, or save it as TXT or structured JSON. Save under each frame renders it at 1280–3840 px, PNG or JPEG, optionally with thirds guides.
  • Export … shots (ZIP): one folder per shot (first and last frame, prompt.txt, shot.json), plus all-prompts.txt, shots.json and a contact sheet.
Tip. Generators work best with clips of 5–10 seconds. For longer shots, split them, or use the first and last frames as start/end images where the generator supports it.
